% ── Abstract ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
Current DNA synthesis screening relies on sequence homology to detect dangerous biological sequences. We show that AI protein design tools, specifically ProteinMPNN, generate functional threat variants with as low as 7\% sequence identity to known threats, completely evading homology-based screening. We introduce FuncScreen, a contrastive learning framework over frozen ESM-2 protein language model embeddings that screens by predicted biological function rather than sequence similarity. Trained with supervised contrastive loss and hard-negative mining on 985 curated pore-forming toxin and benign homolog sequences from UniProt, FuncScreen achieves 1.000 AUROC on standard and hard-negative evaluation splits. On 4,100 ProteinMPNN-designed adversarial variants, FuncScreen maintains 0.991 AUROC where k-mer homology drops to 0.952. At below 20\% sequence identity, homology screening achieves 0\% detection while FuncScreen retains approximately 65\% detection. We further provide the first certified robustness analysis for biosecurity screening under biologically structured mutations, finding an empirical-certified gap of at most 2\%. Our results demonstrate that function-space screening is a necessary complement to sequence-based approaches as AI-assisted biological design becomes more accessible.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

DNA synthesis screening is a critical chokepoint for preventing misuse of synthetic biology. Current screening infrastructure---including SecureDNA \citep{securedna2024} and the IBBIS Common Mechanism \citep{ibbis2024}---fundamentally relies on sequence homology: a query sequence is flagged if it is sufficiently similar to a known threat in a curated database. This approach has served the field well against natural sequences and simple mutations.

However, a new class of threats is emerging. AI protein design tools such as ProteinMPNN \citep{dauparas2022robust} can perform \emph{inverse folding}: given a protein's 3D backbone structure, they generate entirely new amino acid sequences predicted to fold into the same structure---and thus perform the same biological function. These designed sequences can share less than 30\% sequence identity with any naturally occurring protein, falling well below the detection threshold of homology-based screening. The Microsoft cross-sector study \citep{horvitz2025strengthening} demonstrated that AI-designed protein variants already evade existing screening pipelines, and recent ICLR 2026 papers including GeneBreaker and SafeProtein have further characterized this attack surface.

The defensive response to this threat has been notably absent. While the attack side is well-characterized, no published work has built a screening system that operates in \emph{function space} rather than sequence space. This is the gap FuncScreen addresses.

\subsection*{Our main contributions are:}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{FuncScreen}, a contrastive learning framework over frozen ESM-2 \citep{lin2023evolutionary} embeddings that detects threat proteins by functional similarity rather than sequence homology, achieving perfect AUROC on standard and hard-negative splits.
    \item \textbf{A ProteinMPNN adversarial evaluation benchmark} of 4,100 inverse-folded variants across 164 threat proteins at 5 sampling temperatures, providing the first systematic stress test of screening against AI-designed evasion.
    \item \textbf{The first certified robustness analysis for biosecurity screening}, adapting randomized smoothing to biologically structured mutation spaces and quantifying the empirical-certified gap.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\paragraph{DNA synthesis screening.}
SecureDNA \citep{securedna2024} uses cryptographic distributed oblivious PRF to screen sequences as short as 30bp against a curated hazard database. The IBBIS Common Mechanism \citep{ibbis2024} employs HMM-based biorisk screening with best performance above 150bp. Both rely fundamentally on sequence similarity to known threats. The OSTP Framework \citep{ostp2024} established federal guidance requiring compliant providers, and the Biosecurity Modernization and Innovation Act of 2026 (S.3741) mandates screening by gene synthesis providers. However, as \citet{kim2026ai} notes, the legislation mandates homology-based screening without addressing AI-designed functional variants.

\paragraph{AI-assisted evasion of screening.}
\citet{horvitz2025strengthening} demonstrated that AI-designed synthetic protein variants can evade sequence-based screening. GeneBreaker and SafeProtein (both ICLR 2026) further characterize attack surfaces. Edison, Toner \& Esvelt \citep{edison2026assembling} showed that unregulated DNA fragments from dozens of providers are sufficient to assemble dangerous pathogens, demonstrating that fragment-level screening is needed.

\paragraph{Protein language models.}
ESM-2 \citep{lin2023evolutionary} is a 650M-parameter protein language model trained on 65 million sequences from UniRef50. Its learned representations encode functional and structural information beyond raw sequence similarity. ProteinMPNN \citep{dauparas2022robust} performs inverse folding---designing sequences that fold into specified 3D structures---enabling generation of functionally equivalent but sequence-divergent proteins.

\paragraph{Contrastive learning for biological sequences.}
Supervised contrastive learning \citep{khosla2020supervised} has been applied to molecular property prediction and protein function classification, but not to biosecurity screening. Our work is the first to use contrastive learning over PLM embeddings specifically for threat detection.

\paragraph{Certified robustness.}
Randomized smoothing \citep{cohen2019certified} provides provable robustness guarantees for classifiers. RS-Del \citep{huang2023rsdel} extended this to edit-distance perturbations on discrete sequences. We adapt the framework to biologically structured mutation spaces---the first application to biosecurity screening.

\section{Methods}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Data Curation}

We curated 985 protein sequences from UniProt Swiss-Prot (reviewed entries only) organized around \textbf{pore-forming toxins (PFTs)} as our threat family. PFTs were chosen because they are well-annotated, have clear regulatory relevance, and possess rich benign structural homologs that make classification non-trivial.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Threat sequences} (335): pore-forming toxins queried via UniProt keyword KW-0800 (Toxin) combined with free-text ``pore-forming''; aerolysin family (Pfam PF01338); cholesterol-dependent cytolysins (Pfam PF01289); hemolytic toxins (keywords KW-0354 + KW-0800).
    \item \textbf{Hard negatives} (459): MACPF domain proteins (Pfam PF01823) that are \emph{not} toxins---including complement components, perforin, and gasdermin---structurally similar to PFTs but functionally benign; antimicrobial pore-forming peptides.
    \item \textbf{Easy negatives} (191): random human hydrolases with no toxin annotation, providing a baseline for separation difficulty.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Evaluation Splits}

We constructed five evaluation splits designed to progressively stress-test screening:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Standard} (186 sequences): held-out natural threat and benign sequences.
    \item \textbf{Hard negative} (299): threats paired exclusively with structurally similar benign proteins.
    \item \textbf{Sequence divergent} (144): threat sequences with low k-mer similarity to all training threats, testing generalization to naturally distant family members.
    \item \textbf{BLOSUM62 adversarial} (2,242): computationally mutated threat variants using BLOSUM62-conservative substitutions at 5\%--50\% mutation rates.
    \item \textbf{ProteinMPNN adversarial} (4,374): inverse-folded variants generated by ProteinMPNN at 5 sampling temperatures (T=0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.8, 1.0) with 5 sequences per temperature per protein, producing variants with 7\%--60\% sequence identity to source threats. Structures were obtained from AlphaFold DB \citep{jumper2021highly}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Embedding Extraction}

We use ESM-2 (650M parameters, \texttt{facebook/esm2\_t33\_650M\_UR50D}) as a frozen feature extractor. For each protein sequence, we extract the last hidden state, exclude CLS and EOS tokens, and mean-pool over sequence positions to obtain a 1,280-dimensional embedding. All embeddings are pre-computed and stored for downstream use.

\subsection{FuncScreen: Contrastive Screening Model}

FuncScreen consists of a \textbf{projection head} and a \textbf{classification head} trained jointly on frozen ESM-2 embeddings.

The \textbf{projection head} maps 1,280-dim embeddings to a 256-dim normalized space via three linear layers with batch normalization, ReLU activation, and dropout (0.1). The output is L2-normalized, placing all projections on a unit hypersphere.

The \textbf{classification head} takes the 256-dim projection and produces a scalar threat probability via two linear layers with ReLU and dropout.

\paragraph{Training objective.} We use a combined loss:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = \alpha \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{SupCon}} + (1 - \alpha) \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{SupCon}}$ is the supervised contrastive loss \citep{khosla2020supervised} with temperature $\tau = 0.07$, $\mathcal{L}_{\text{BCE}}$ is binary cross-entropy, and $\alpha = 0.5$. The contrastive loss pulls together embeddings of the same class (threat or benign) and pushes apart different classes, while BCE provides direct classification signal.

\paragraph{Hard-negative mining.} For each threat protein in a batch, we identify the $k=3$ closest benign proteins in embedding space (by cosine similarity) and oversample them in training. This forces the model to learn a tight decision boundary in the region where threats and benign homologs are most easily confused.

\paragraph{Training details.} AdamW optimizer, learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, cosine annealing schedule, batch size 64, 50 epochs. Best model selected by validation AUROC (0.9998).

\subsection{Baseline Methods}

We compare against four baselines representing the spectrum from traditional to embedding-based screening:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{K-mer similarity} ($k=5$): maximum Jaccard similarity of 5-mer sets between query and known threats. Proxy for BLAST/homology screening.
    \item \textbf{Cosine NN}: mean cosine similarity to the 5 nearest threat embeddings in ESM-2 space. Represents naive embedding retrieval.
    \item \textbf{Linear classifier}: logistic regression on ESM-2 embeddings with balanced class weights.
    \item \textbf{KNN classifier}: 5-nearest-neighbor classifier on cosine-normalized ESM-2 embeddings.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Certified Robustness}

We adapt randomized smoothing \citep{cohen2019certified} to biological mutation spaces. Given a base classifier $f$, we construct a smoothed classifier $g$ by:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item Applying $k$ random mutations to the input sequence (either \emph{conservative} within Dayhoff amino acid groups \citep{dayhoff1978model}, or \emph{unrestricted}).
    \item Re-embedding the mutated sequence through ESM-2.
    \item Classifying via the contrastive model.
    \item Repeating for $n=200$ Monte Carlo samples and taking the majority vote.
\end{enumerate}

We certify each prediction using a one-sided Clopper-Pearson bound on the majority class probability: if $p_{\text{lower}} > 0.5$ at significance level $\alpha = 0.001$, the prediction is certified robust.

\subsection{Sequence-Divergent Threats Expose Homology Screening}

On the sequence-divergent split (\Cref{tab:main_results}, column 3), k-mer homology screening drops to \textbf{0.821 AUROC}---a 17-point decline from the standard split and a catastrophic failure for operational screening. Cosine NN retrieval in ESM-2 space partially recovers (0.886) but still falls short. FuncScreen achieves \textbf{0.974 AUROC}, a +15.3 percentage point improvement over homology and +8.8 over naive embeddings, demonstrating that the learned contrastive boundary generalizes to distant threat family members.

\subsection{BLOSUM62 Mutations Do Not Evade Screening}

On the BLOSUM62 adversarial split (conservative amino acid substitutions at 5--50\% mutation rates), all methods achieve $>$0.98 AUROC (\Cref{tab:main_results}). This indicates that simple point mutations---even at high rates---do not displace sequences far enough in embedding space to evade function-aware screening. The implication: \textbf{an adversary needs AI protein design tools, not manual mutation, to meaningfully evade screening.}

\Cref{fig:certified} presents empirical versus certified accuracy under biologically structured mutations. Across mutation budgets from $k=1$ to $k=10$ and both conservative (Dayhoff group) and random substitutions, the \textbf{empirical-certified gap is at most 2\%}. The certification rate exceeds 98\% in all configurations, with mean $p_{\text{lower}} > 0.94$.

Conservative and random substitutions produce nearly identical robustness profiles, suggesting the model is insensitive to the specific mutation mechanism.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Our results establish that function-aware screening in protein language model embedding space is a viable and necessary complement to sequence-based approaches. The key insight is not that PLM embeddings are useful for classification---this is well-known---but that \textbf{contrastive learning with hard-negative mining produces a screening boundary that remains discriminative at sequence identity levels where all homology-based methods fail}.

The ProteinMPNN adversarial benchmark is, to our knowledge, the first systematic evaluation of screening against AI-designed inverse-folded variants. The finding that homology screening achieves 0\% detection below 20\% identity is not surprising in isolation, but quantifying this against a concrete, reproducible adversarial pipeline provides the evidence base needed to motivate infrastructure investment in function-aware screening.

The certified robustness analysis addresses a different concern: not adversarial evasion, but operational stability. A screening system that changes its verdict under minor biological mutations (e.g., natural polymorphisms between strains) is operationally unreliable. Our certification results show that FuncScreen's decisions are stable, with provable guarantees.

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Scope}: we evaluated on one threat family (pore-forming toxins). Generalization to other regulated families (e.g., Select Agent toxins, viral polymerases) requires additional curation and validation.
    \item \textbf{Detection at extreme divergence}: FuncScreen's detection rate drops to $\sim$65\% below 20\% sequence identity. While this far exceeds all baselines (0\%), it is not sufficient for standalone deployment. Function-aware screening should be a \emph{layer} in a multi-modal screening stack, not a replacement.
    \item \textbf{KNN baseline competitiveness}: the KNN baseline achieves 0.997 AUROC on the MPNN adversarial split versus FuncScreen's 0.991. In aggregate metrics, simple nearest-neighbor approaches are competitive. FuncScreen's advantage is concentrated at the critical low-identity regime.
    \item \textbf{Certification scope}: certified robustness was evaluated on 50 sequences with 200 Monte Carlo samples. Production-grade certification would require larger sample sizes (Cohen et al. recommend 100,000+).
    \item \textbf{No ProTrek comparison}: the plan included ProTrek tri-modal embeddings as a potentially stronger backbone. Installation constraints prevented this comparison.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Future Work}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Multi-family evaluation}: extend to all OSTP-regulated threat categories.
    \item \textbf{ProTrek integration}: tri-modal (sequence + structure + function text) embeddings may improve detection at extreme divergence.
    \item \textbf{Integration with SecureDNA/IBBIS}: function-aware scoring as an additional signal in existing screening pipelines.
    \item \textbf{Adversarial training}: incorporate ProteinMPNN-designed variants into training to harden the decision boundary.
    \item \textbf{Nucleotide-level screening}: extend from protein to DNA sequence screening, bridging to operational synthesis order review.
\end{itemize}

% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\section{Conclusion}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

We introduced FuncScreen, a contrastive learning framework over ESM-2 embeddings for biosecurity screening that operates in function space rather than sequence space. FuncScreen achieves perfect AUROC on standard evaluation, +15.3 points over homology on sequence-divergent threats, and maintains $\sim$65\% detection on ProteinMPNN-designed variants where all homology methods achieve 0\%. Certified robustness analysis confirms that screening decisions are provably stable under biological mutations.

As AI protein design tools become more accessible, the gap between what generative models can design and what screening systems can detect will widen. FuncScreen demonstrates that protein language model embeddings, combined with contrastive learning and hard-negative mining, can significantly narrow this gap. We release all code, data, and trained models to support further development of function-aware biosecurity infrastructure.

\section{Limitations and Dual-Use Considerations}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\paragraph{Limitations.}
FuncScreen is scoped to one threat family (pore-forming toxins). False positive rates on out-of-distribution protein families have not been characterized. The adversarial evaluation uses ProteinMPNN, which generates sequences optimized for structural compatibility but does not experimentally verify biological function. Detection degrades at extreme sequence divergence ($<$20\% identity), indicating that function-aware screening alone is not sufficient.

\paragraph{Dual-use risks.}
Our adversarial evaluation pipeline (ProteinMPNN variant generation + screening evasion quantification) could theoretically inform an adversary about screening blind spots. We mitigate this by: (a) focusing on \emph{defensive} capability development, (b) using only publicly known threat sequences from UniProt, (c) not providing specific evasion recipes, and (d) releasing the screening tool alongside the evaluation to enable immediate defensive deployment.

\paragraph{Responsible disclosure.}
The vulnerability we characterize---homology screening failure against AI-designed variants---is already known from published work \citep{horvitz2025strengthening}. Our contribution is the first \emph{defense}, not a new attack.

\paragraph{Ethical considerations.}
All threat sequences used are from public databases of naturally occurring proteins. No novel dangerous sequences were designed. ProteinMPNN variants are computational predictions with no experimental validation of function. The project was designed to strengthen, not weaken, biosecurity infrastructure.

\paragraph{Future improvements.}
Multi-family evaluation, integration with operational screening pipelines (SecureDNA, IBBIS), adversarial training on AI-designed variants, and experimental validation of detection on wet-lab-verified functional variants.
